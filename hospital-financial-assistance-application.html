How to Apply for Hospital Financial Assistance

Last reviewed 2026-11-02 · How-to guides
Short answerTo apply for hospital financial assistance, find the hospital's written financial assistance policy, often on its website, check the eligibility rules, gather income and identity documents, and submit the application before paying or setting up a plan. Tax-exempt hospitals must have such a policy under Internal Revenue Code § 501(r).

What is hospital financial assistance?

Financial assistance, sometimes called charity care, is a program where a hospital discounts or waives part of a bill for patients who meet its criteria. It is separate from insurance. Each hospital sets its own eligibility rules, so the same income may qualify at one hospital and not at another.

What does 501(r) require of nonprofit hospitals?

Tax-exempt hospital organizations are covered by Internal Revenue Code § 501(r). In general terms, they must do the following:

  • Have a written financial assistance policy
  • Limit charges to patients who are eligible for assistance
  • Make reasonable efforts to determine eligibility before taking extraordinary collection actions

How do you find the policy?

Start by finding out whether the hospital is a nonprofit, which the billing office or the hospital's website can tell you. Then search the site for terms like financial assistance, charity care, or billing help. The policy, plain-language summary, and application are often posted together. If you cannot find them, call the billing office or patient financial services and ask.

What documents are commonly requested?

Requirements vary, but applications often ask for some combination of the following:

  • Proof of income, such as recent pay stubs or a tax return
  • Identification and proof of address
  • Household size
  • Bank statements or other proof of assets, if the policy asks for them
  • Your insurer's EOB or denial letter, if you have insurance
  • A copy of the bill you want reviewed

What if you already have a bill or collection notice?

Apply as soon as you can and tell the billing office you have applied. Ask them to pause collection activity while the application is reviewed, and get that in writing. For a nonprofit hospital, you can also ask how it determines eligibility before taking extraordinary collection actions.

Fix any billing errors separately, since a corrected bill changes the amount being reviewed.

What if you are denied?

Ask for the reason in writing and whether an appeal or a second review is available. Check that every page of the application was received and that the documents were accepted. If the hospital is tax-exempt and you believe its policy was not followed, your state attorney general's consumer office may be a place to raise it.

Step-by-step

  1. Confirm the hospital typeAsk whether the hospital is a nonprofit, since tax-exempt hospitals must follow 501(r) rules.
  2. Find the policy and formSearch the hospital's website or call patient financial services for the policy and application.
  3. Check eligibilityRead the income and other criteria so you know what documents to expect.
  4. Gather documentsCollect proof of income, identification, household information, and a copy of the bill.
  5. Complete every sectionFill out the form fully and avoid leaving blanks that could delay review.
  6. Submit and get confirmationDeliver the application in a way that gives you a receipt, and keep a complete copy.
  7. Ask for a collections holdRequest that the account be paused while the application is reviewed.
  8. Follow up or appealCheck on the status, and ask about an appeal if the answer is no.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to be uninsured to get financial assistance?

Not necessarily. Policies differ, and some also consider patients who have insurance but still face a large remaining balance. Read the hospital's policy or ask patient financial services how it treats insured patients, and bring your EOB with you.

Can I apply after I already received the bill?

You can ask. Hospitals set their own application timeframes, so apply as soon as you can and ask what window applies to your account. If the bill is already with a collector, tell both the hospital and the collector that an application is pending.

Does a for-profit hospital have to offer financial assistance?

The 501(r) rules discussed here apply to tax-exempt hospital organizations. A for-profit hospital may still have its own program, so ask the billing office. If it does not, you can still ask about discounts and payment plans.

Can the hospital send me to collections while my application is pending?

Policies differ. For tax-exempt hospitals, 501(r) requires reasonable efforts to determine eligibility before extraordinary collection actions. Ask the hospital in writing to hold the account during review, and keep proof of the date you applied.
