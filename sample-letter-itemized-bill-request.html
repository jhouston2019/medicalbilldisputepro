Sample Letter Requesting an Itemized Medical Bill

Last reviewed 2026-11-02 · Sample letters
Short answerAn itemized bill request letter asks the provider for a line-by-line statement showing each service, date, quantity, billing code and charge. Send it in writing, include your account number and date of service, ask the provider to pause collection while you review, and request a reply within 10 business days.

Why ask for an itemized bill first?

A summary statement often shows only a total, or broad categories such as pharmacy or room and board. You cannot spot a repeated charge, a wrong quantity or a service you never received from a total. An itemized bill breaks the balance into individual lines.

For many disputes this letter is the first step, sent before any accusation. It is neutral, it is easy for a billing office to grant, and it gives you the material for everything that follows.

What should the letter ask for?

Be specific, because billing offices respond to what is requested. Asking only for a copy of the bill may get you the same summary again.

  • Each service or supply with its date and description
  • The quantity or units for each line
  • The billing code attached to each line
  • The charge for each line, plus any adjustments, insurance payments and write-offs
  • The names of the providers or departments that billed each item
  • A statement of the current balance and what part is patient responsibility

How do you customize and send it?

Fill in your account number, the date or dates of service, and the address of the billing department as printed on your statement. If you were treated at a hospital, remember that doctors may bill separately, so send one request to each billing office that has contacted you.

Certified mail, a patient portal message or a fax with a confirmation page all create a record. Note the date you sent it and keep a copy.

What should you check when it arrives?

Lay the itemized bill next to your EOB. The EOB shows what the provider submitted and what the insurer allowed. The totals should line up with the charges, adjustments and your patient responsibility.

Look for the common problems explained on the related pages: repeated lines, quantities that seem too high, charges on dates you were not there, services bundled and billed separately, and items you do not recognize.

  • Same item listed twice on one date
  • Quantity or units that do not match what you received
  • Charges dated before or after your visit
  • Items you cannot match to your care
  • Amounts billed to you that the EOB shows as insurer-paid or written off

What if they do not respond?

Follow up by phone after 10 business days and write down who you spoke to. Ask for a supervisor if needed. If the account is approaching collections, tell the office that you are waiting on the itemized bill and request a written hold. Sample letter

[YOUR NAME]
[YOUR ADDRESS]
[CITY, STATE ZIP]
[PHONE / EMAIL]

[DATE]

[PROVIDER NAME]
Attn: Billing Department
[PROVIDER BILLING ADDRESS]

Re: Request for itemized bill
Patient: [PATIENT NAME]
Account number: [ACCOUNT NUMBER]
Date(s) of service: [DATE OF SERVICE]
Current balance shown: [AMOUNT]

To the Billing Department:

I received a statement for the account above and would like to review the charges before making payment. The statement I received [DOES NOT LIST INDIVIDUAL SERVICES / SHOWS ONLY A TOTAL]. Please send me a complete itemized bill for the dates of service listed.

The itemized bill should include, for each line:

1. The date of service and a description of the service or supply
2. The quantity or units
3. The billing code
4. The charge, along with all insurance payments, adjustments and write-offs
5. The name of the department or provider that billed the item

Please also tell me the current patient-responsibility balance after all insurance activity.

Because I am reviewing the charges, I ask that you place this account on hold and not refer it to a collection agency until I have had time to review the itemized statement. Please confirm the hold in writing.

I would appreciate a response within 10 business days of your receipt of this letter. You may send the itemized bill to the address above or to [EMAIL ADDRESS].

Thank you for your help.

Sincerely,

[YOUR NAME]
[YOUR PHONE NUMBER]

Replace every [BRACKETED] placeholder before sending, and keep a copy plus proof of delivery.

Frequently asked questions

Is a provider required to give me an itemized bill?

Providers generally can supply one, and most will when asked. Requirements can vary by state and by your plan, so check your state regulator's site if you are refused. You may also have a right to certain billing records under HIPAA, which generally requires a response within 30 days.

Will asking for an itemized bill delay my insurance claim?

It should not affect the claim already filed. It does not stop your insurer from processing, but it does not automatically stop collection either, so ask in writing that the account be held. Keep your due dates in view while you wait.

What is the difference between an itemized bill and an EOB?

An itemized bill comes from the provider and lists the services and charges. An EOB comes from your insurer and explains how a claim was processed. The EOB is not a bill. You compare the two to see whether what you are asked to pay matches what the plan decided.

Do I have to pay while I wait for the itemized bill?

That depends on your situation. Many people ask the billing office to place the account on hold while they review, and confirm that in writing. If you decide to pay a portion, record the date and amount, and note that the rest is under review.
