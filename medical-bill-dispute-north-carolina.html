Disputing a Medical Bill in North Carolina, Including Self-Pay and Medicaid

Three kinds of patients, three starting points

Your coverage status changes the tools you have. Find yourself below.

  • Privately insured: compare your EOB with the bill and use the plan's appeal if the claim was denied.
  • Uninsured or self-pay: use the good faith estimate rules and financial assistance.
  • Medicaid: providers who accept Medicaid generally may not bill beyond the Medicaid payment and any allowed copay.

If you are uninsured or paying yourself

Uninsured and self-pay patients are entitled to a good faith estimate before scheduled care. If the final bill is $400 or more above the estimate from that provider or facility, you may start the federal patient-provider dispute resolution process. The filing window is short, so check the CMS website for the current deadline instead of guessing.

Keep the estimate with your records. Without it, you have little to compare against.

Negotiating a self-pay balance

Self-pay balances often have more room for discussion than insured ones. Ask for an itemized bill first, check it for errors, and then ask about financial assistance, prompt-pay discounts and payment plans. Get any agreement in writing before you pay.

If you have Medicaid

A provider who accepts Medicaid generally may not bill you for amounts beyond the Medicaid payment and any allowed copay. If you receive a bill for the balance, contact the state Medicaid agency and ask the provider to confirm in writing whether it billed Medicaid. Say "generally" in your own notes, because exceptions exist, and the agency can confirm what applies to you.

North Carolina offices

The North Carolina Department of Insurance takes complaints about insurers and the plans it regulates. The Attorney General's consumer protection office takes concerns about unfair or deceptive billing practices. For possible federal surprise billing violations, call the CMS No Surprises Help Desk at 1-800-985-3059. State law may add protections, so check the department's site.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good faith estimate?

It is a written estimate of expected charges that uninsured and self-pay patients are entitled to receive before scheduled care. If your final bill is $400 or more above the estimate from that provider or facility, you may use the federal dispute process.

How long do I have to start the federal dispute process?

The window is short, and this page does not state a number. Check the CMS No Surprises information page for the current deadline as soon as you notice a large gap between the estimate and the bill.

Can a provider bill me the difference if I have Medicaid?

Providers who accept Medicaid generally may not bill you beyond the Medicaid payment and any allowed copay. If you receive such a bill, contact the state Medicaid agency and ask the provider to confirm what it billed to Medicaid.

Who handles insurance complaints in North Carolina?

The North Carolina Department of Insurance takes consumer complaints about insurers. Self-funded employer plans are generally outside state insurance regulators' reach, so check your plan documents if you are unsure which route to use.
