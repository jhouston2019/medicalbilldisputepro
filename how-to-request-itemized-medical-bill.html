How to Request an Itemized Medical Bill

Last reviewed 2026-11-02 · How-to guides
Short answerTo request an itemized medical bill, contact the provider's billing office and ask for a line-by-line statement showing each service, date, quantity, and charge. Put the request in writing, include your account number, and keep proof of when you sent it. Then compare each line with your insurer's EOB before paying.

What is the difference between an itemized bill and a summary bill?

A summary bill shows a total and maybe a few categories, such as room charges or pharmacy. An itemized bill lists each service, supply, and medication separately, with a date, a quantity, and a charge. Only the itemized version lets you spot a repeated line or a quantity that looks off.

Hospitals often send a summary first, so asking for the itemized version is routine. It is not an accusation.

How do you ask for it?

A phone call is a fine start, but follow it with something written so you have a record. Because billing records may be part of your designated record set, the HIPAA right of access (45 C.F.R. § 164.524) can also support your request, and providers generally must respond within 30 days.

  • Call the billing office and ask for a fully itemized statement for your dates of service
  • Give your name, date of birth, account number, and dates of service
  • Ask to receive it by mail or through the patient portal
  • Ask how long it will take and write down the answer
  • Send a short written follow-up and save a copy

What should a useful itemized bill show?

If the statement leaves out dates or descriptions, ask for a more detailed version. Codes can be hard to read, and you can ask the billing office to explain any line in plain language.

  • The date of service for each line
  • A description of each service or supply
  • Quantity or units
  • The charge for each line and the total
  • Billing codes, where the provider uses them
  • Payments, insurance adjustments, and the remaining balance

What should you check on the itemized bill?

Start with the dates, then look for repeated lines, supplies you do not remember, and quantities that seem high, such as several units of something given once. Compare the total with the patient responsibility on your EOB.

Hospital stays can involve facility charges plus separate bills from physician groups, so a charge that seems to be missing may arrive on another statement.

Can hospital price information help?

Hospitals must publish their standard charges under federal price transparency rules (45 C.F.R. Part 180), including a consumer-friendly display of shoppable services. That information can give you a reference point when a charge looks surprising, though it does not replace the bill itself.

Should you hold off on paying while you wait?

It is reasonable to ask the provider to pause collection activity until you receive and review the itemized statement. Say so in your request and note the response. If a due date is printed on the bill, mention it and ask that the account be marked as under review.

Step-by-step

  1. Find your account detailsHave your account number, dates of service, and the billing office contact information in front of you.
  2. Ask for an itemized statementCall or use the patient portal and ask for a line-by-line statement covering every date of service.
  3. Follow up in writingSend a short message or letter repeating the request, and keep a copy with the date.
  4. Ask for a pauseRequest that the account not move to collections while you wait for the statement.
  5. Review dates and quantitiesCheck each line against the dates you received care and the amounts you remember.
  6. Compare with your EOBMatch the totals to the patient responsibility shown by your insurer.
  7. Dispute what does not matchRaise any mismatched, repeated, or unexplained line with the billing office in writing.

Frequently asked questions

Is a hospital required to give me an itemized bill?

Many providers will provide one on request, and billing records can be part of the records you have a right to request under HIPAA. If a provider is slow to respond, put the request in writing, mention the right of access, and keep proof. Your state regulator's site may list additional rules.

Will I have to pay for an itemized bill?

Some providers supply itemized statements at no charge, while others may charge fees for copies of records. Ask before you request, and ask whether a patient-portal download is available at no cost.

What if the itemized bill has codes I do not understand?

Ask the billing office to explain each code in plain language, and compare the descriptions with your EOB. You do not need to decode everything yourself. Focus on whether each service happened, whether it appears once, and whether the amount matches what your insurer says you owe.

Can I request an itemized bill for an old visit?

You can always ask. Providers keep records for varying lengths of time, so the sooner you ask the better. If the account is already with a collector, you can also ask for verification of the debt in writing.
