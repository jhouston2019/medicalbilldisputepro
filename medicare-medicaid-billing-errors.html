Why do these programs follow different rules?

Medicare and Medicaid are government programs with their own billing rules, notices and appeal processes. The federal No Surprises Act does not apply to Medicare, Medicaid, TRICARE, VA or Indian Health Service coverage, so the protections that apply to private plans may not work the same way for you.

That does not mean you have no protection. It means you use different documents and different offices. The first step is to know which program actually covered the care.

How do you check a Medicare bill?

The Medicare Summary Notice (MSN) is Medicare's statement of what was billed, what Medicare approved and what you may owe. It is not a bill. Compare it line by line with the provider's statement.

  • Dates of service and the provider's name.
  • Services listed that you did not receive.
  • The same service appearing twice.
  • The amount the provider says you owe compared with the amount on the MSN.
  • Whether Medicare shows a payment the provider ignored.
  • Whether you have supplemental coverage that should have been billed.

How can you appeal under Medicare?

Medicare has an appeal process for claims decisions. The deadline is on your notice, so read it and act before it passes. The Medicare website has information on claims, appeals and complaints, and you can call 1-800-MEDICARE through the information listed there.

If you suspect a billing mistake rather than a coverage decision, start by asking the provider to review and correct the claim. Keep your MSN and any written response from the provider.

What are the limits for Medicaid patients?

Providers who accept Medicaid generally may not bill the patient for amounts beyond the Medicaid payment and any allowed copay (42 C.F.R. § 447.15). If you received a bill larger than a small copay for a Medicaid-covered service, ask the provider to explain and to verify your coverage on the date of service.

Say generally, not always. Details depend on your state program and on whether the provider accepted Medicaid for your care. Contact your state Medicaid agency, which can tell you what applies.

How do you write the dispute?

Describe the service, the date, the amount and why you believe the bill is wrong. Attach your MSN or Medicaid notice and ask for a written response. Many providers and collectors expect disputes within a short window printed on the bill, so act promptly and keep proof of when you wrote.

Frequently asked questions

Is a Medicare Summary Notice a bill?

No. The MSN is Medicare's statement of the claims it processed, the amounts approved and what you may owe the provider. Compare it with the provider's bill. If the provider's amount is higher than the MSN shows you owe, ask the provider to explain and correct the account.

Can a Medicaid provider bill me for the rest of the charge?

Providers who accept Medicaid generally may not bill you for amounts beyond the Medicaid payment and any allowed copay. Exceptions and details depend on the state program and the provider's status. If you receive a larger bill, contact your state Medicaid agency and the provider's billing office.

What if I had Medicare and a private plan?

Coverage order matters, and claims may need to go to one payer before another. If a bill seems wrong, ask the provider which plan it billed first and request an itemized statement. Compare it with both your Medicare Summary Notice and the private plan's EOB.

How long do I have to appeal a Medicare decision?

The deadline is on your notice, and it can differ by the type of decision. Read the notice carefully and do not rely on a number from a general article. If you are unsure, the Medicare website and 1-800-MEDICARE can explain which appeal applies and when to file.

Who can help me with Medicare or Medicaid billing errors?

For Medicare, start with the provider's billing office and then Medicare through medicare.gov. For Medicaid, contact your state Medicaid agency. If a collector contacts you, you can dispute the debt in writing under the FDCPA, and the CFPB accepts complaints about debt collectors.
