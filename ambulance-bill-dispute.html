Disputing a ground ambulance bill

Why are ground ambulance bills so confusing?

You rarely choose an ambulance. Whoever responds to the call takes you to the hospital, and that company may or may not be in your insurer's network. Afterward you may receive a bill for the full charge, with only part of it paid by insurance.

The important limit to understand is that ground ambulance services are not covered by the federal No Surprises Act. Air ambulance services are treated differently, so check that page if you were flown. For ground transport, your help comes from your plan's terms, state law, and negotiation.

What should you check on the bill and the EOB?

Request an itemized bill, then compare it with your EOB. Look at how the claim was processed and what the insurer lists as your responsibility.

  • Whether the base charge, mileage and any supplies are listed separately and make sense for the trip.
  • The pickup and drop-off locations and the date of service.
  • Whether the insurer treated the ambulance as in-network or out-of-network.
  • Whether the claim was denied, and if so the reason printed on the EOB, such as a coverage or medical necessity issue.
  • Whether you are billed twice, once by the ambulance company and once by a billing contractor.

How can you use your health plan's appeal process?

If your insurer denied the claim or paid less than you expected, you can appeal through the plan's internal process, and you may then request an independent external review. The deadline is printed on your denial notice or EOB. Many plans allow roughly six months, but check yours.

In an appeal, explain that the transport was needed, attach the ambulance run report if you can get it, and ask the insurer to reprocess the claim. A letter from the treating hospital or physician describing the emergency can help.

What can state law and regulators do?

Some states regulate ambulance billing, and some local governments set rates or contract with specific services. Because rules vary, check your state insurance department's and attorney general's websites rather than assuming a rule applies. The NAIC state map at content.naic.org lists each state's insurance regulator.

Complaints about how an insurer handled a claim generally go to the state insurance department. Complaints about the ambulance company's billing practices may go to the state attorney general.

How do you negotiate when the charge stands?

When an ambulance bill is accurate but unaffordable, you can still ask for help. Call the ambulance company and ask whether it offers a self-pay discount, a financial hardship program or a payment plan. Get any agreement in writing before you pay.

Frequently asked questions

Does the No Surprises Act cover ground ambulances?

No. The federal No Surprises Act covers air ambulance services but not ground ambulance services. That means an out-of-network ground ambulance can still bill you under your plan's terms, though state law may add protections. Ask your state insurance department what applies where you live.

Can I refuse to pay an ambulance bill if it was an emergency?

Needing the ambulance in an emergency does not by itself cancel the bill. You can dispute errors, appeal how your insurer processed it, and ask for a reduction or payment plan. Ignoring the bill can lead to collections, so respond in writing and keep a record of every contact.

Why did my insurer deny my ambulance claim?

The EOB should list a reason. Common reasons include a claim the plan considered not medically necessary, missing information, or a transport the plan treats as out-of-network. Read the reason, then use the plan's appeal process and ask the ambulance company to resubmit the claim if information was missing.

What should I ask the ambulance company for?

Ask for an itemized bill, the date and locations of the trip, the name of the billing entity, and a copy of the run report if available. Also ask whether it accepted your insurance and whether it offers discounts or payment plans. Request answers in writing.

Is there a federal agency that can help with an ambulance bill?

The CMS No Surprises Help Desk handles questions about the federal law, but ground ambulances fall outside it. Your state insurance department and attorney general are usually better starting points, and the CFPB accepts complaints if the bill goes to a debt collector.
