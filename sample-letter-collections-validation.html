Sample Debt Validation Letter to a Medical Collector

Last reviewed 2026-11-02 · Sample letters
Short answerA debt validation letter is a written dispute sent to a third-party collector. Under the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, if you dispute in writing within 30 days of receiving the validation notice, the collector must stop collection until it verifies the debt. Send it by trackable mail and keep a copy.

When does a validation letter apply?

Use this letter when a third-party collection agency or debt buyer contacts you about a medical bill. Under 15 U.S.C. § 1692g, a debt collector must send a validation notice, and a consumer who disputes the debt in writing within the notice period, which is 30 days from receipt, can require the collector to stop collecting until it verifies the debt.

The law generally applies to third-party collectors, not to the original provider collecting its own bill. If the hospital itself is calling, send a regular dispute letter instead.

How should you time and send it?

Find the date on the validation notice and the date it arrived, and mark the 30-day mark on your calendar. Aim to mail the letter well before it. A phone call does not replace a written dispute, so write even if you have already spoken to the collector.

Use certified mail with a return receipt, or another method that gives you proof of delivery. Keep a copy of the letter and the receipt together with the original notice.

What should the letter say?

Keep it short and neutral. Identify the account by the number on the collector's notice, state that you dispute the debt and request validation, and ask for specific documents. Do not admit that the debt is yours, and do not promise payment in this letter.

  • The name of the original provider and the date of service
  • An itemization of the amount claimed, including any interest or fees
  • Proof that the collector is authorized to collect this debt
  • A copy of the original itemized bill
  • A request to stop collection activity until validation is provided

What can you do beyond the letter?

Also write to the original provider, and compare its bill with your EOB. Sometimes the debt was sent to collections while an insurance appeal or a billing correction was still pending. If so, say so in both letters and attach the evidence.

If the collector ignores your letter or continues to contact you after you disputed, you can submit a complaint to the CFPB at consumerfinance.gov. Credit bureau policies on medical collections have changed over time, so check current rules there rather than relying on older articles.

What to expect

The letter asks for a response in 10 business days. The collector may send documentation, close the account, or return it to the provider. Read whatever arrives closely, and compare it to your own records. Medical Bill Dispute Pro focuses on disputes with providers, so for an active collection involving a lawsuit or a large balance, consider a healthcare or consumer attorney.

Sample letter

[YOUR NAME]
[YOUR ADDRESS]
[CITY, STATE ZIP]

[DATE]

[COLLECTION AGENCY NAME]
[COLLECTION AGENCY ADDRESS]

Sent by certified mail, return receipt requested

Re: Dispute and request for validation of debt
Collector reference / account number: [ACCOUNT NUMBER]
Original creditor: [PROVIDER NAME]
Date of service: [DATE OF SERVICE]
Amount claimed: [AMOUNT]

To Whom It May Concern:

I received your notice dated [DATE OF NOTICE] on [DATE RECEIVED] regarding the account above. I dispute this debt in its entirety, and I request validation under the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1692g.

Please provide the following:

1. The name and address of the original creditor and the date of service
2. An itemized accounting of the amount claimed, including any interest, fees or other charges added
3. A copy of the original itemized bill
4. Documentation showing that your company is authorized to collect this debt

Please stop collection activity on this account until you have mailed me this verification. Please also do not report the account as undisputed to any credit reporting agency while the dispute is open.

This letter is not an acknowledgment that I owe the debt, and it is not a promise to pay. Please send your response in writing within 10 business days of receipt.

Sincerely,

[YOUR NAME]
[YOUR PHONE NUMBER]

Replace every [BRACKETED] placeholder before sending, and keep a copy plus proof of delivery.

Frequently asked questions

What if more than 30 days have passed?

You can still write, and a collector may still respond. But the special protection of stopping collection until verification applies to disputes made within the notice period. Send your letter as soon as you can, and consider speaking to a consumer attorney or a nonprofit credit counselor.

Does sending the letter admit that I owe the debt?

A letter that disputes the debt and asks for validation does not say that you owe it. Avoid statements such as 'I will pay' or 'I owe', and avoid sending payment with the letter unless you have decided to resolve the account.

Should I pay a small amount to make them stop?

Be careful. Depending on your state, a payment may affect your rights, and some people want to confirm the debt first. Ask for validation before paying anything, and keep records of every contact.

Can I tell the collector to stop calling?

You can put a request about contact in writing. The CFPB website explains the options available to consumers, and complaints about collectors can be filed there. Keep notes on the dates and times of any calls you receive.

What if the collector is the hospital's own billing department?

The FDCPA generally applies to third-party collectors, so it may not cover the original provider collecting its own bill. Use the standard dispute or itemized-bill letters instead, and ask about financial assistance if you qualify.
