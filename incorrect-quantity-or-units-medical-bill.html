Wrong Date, Quantity, Units, or Patient on a Medical Bill: How to Catch Errors

Last reviewed 2026-11-02 · Billing errors
Short answerClerical errors on a bill include wrong dates of service, incorrect quantities or units, the wrong patient, or the wrong provider. To catch them, request an itemized bill, compare each line to your records and EOB, and flag mismatches in dates, counts, and units. Then ask billing to correct them in writing.

What do clerical billing errors look like?

These are the small mistakes that are easy to miss because they hide inside a long itemized list. Each one can change what you owe or cause your insurer to deny a claim.

  • A date of service that does not match the day you were treated
  • A quantity that is too high, such as 10 units of a medication when you received 1
  • Units that do not match how the service is measured, such as time-based units counted wrongly
  • Another patient's name, birth date, or insurance ID on your account
  • A provider or department you did not see
  • A charge dated after you were discharged

Why do quantity and units matter so much?

Many charges are a price multiplied by a quantity. As an illustration, if a $25 supply is billed with a quantity of 8 and you used 1, the line shows $200. The unit price looks reasonable, so the error is easy to miss.

Some services are billed by units of time or by item, which adds room for confusion. If a unit does not make sense to you, ask billing what the unit means and how it was counted.

How do you check the top of the bill first?

Before reading any line items, verify the basics at the top of the statement. Confirm your full name and date of birth, your insurance member ID, the account number, the facility name, and the dates of service. Mixed-up identifying details can attach someone else's charges to your account or cause your insurer to reject the claim.

If anything here is wrong, tell billing right away and ask them to correct it, since errors in these fields can affect every line below.

How do you check each line?

Request an itemized bill if you only have a summary. Then compare it with your EOB, the after-visit summary, and the patient portal. Take it line by line.

  • Does each date match a visit you can confirm?
  • Does each quantity match what you remember or what the records show?
  • Do the descriptions match the care you received?
  • Does the EOB list the same dates, services, and counts?
  • Are there lines you cannot explain at all?

How do you get an error corrected?

Call billing and point to the specific line: the date of service shows one date but I was treated on a different day. Then follow up in writing with the account number, the line, what you believe is correct, and your supporting documents. Ask for a corrected itemized statement within 10 business days.

If your insurer already processed the claim, ask the provider to submit a corrected claim. A date or patient error on the original claim can lead to a denial, and the correction may need to reach the insurer, not only your account.

What if the provider says the entry is correct?

Ask for the documentation behind it, such as the order, time stamps, or the record of the visit. If the records support the entry, you will at least know why. If they do not, ask for a supervisor review. You can also contact your state insurance department or attorney general if the provider will not engage.

Frequently asked questions

How common are billing errors like wrong dates or quantities?

There is no number you can rely on, but clerical errors are a recognized reason for disputes, and itemized bills are long enough that mistakes can slip through. The practical answer is to check your own bill line by line instead of assuming it is right, especially for larger charges.

What should I do if another patient's information is on my bill?

Contact billing right away and ask them to correct the account. Do not share additional personal details beyond what is needed to verify your identity. You can also ask the provider's privacy office to review it, since it may involve your health information or another patient's.

Can a wrong date of service cause my insurance to deny the claim?

It can. If the date, patient details, or coverage dates on a claim do not match, the insurer may deny it. Ask the provider to submit a corrected claim and ask your insurer to reprocess it. Check the denial notice for the appeal deadline.

What does units mean on an itemized bill?

Units are how a service or item is counted for billing, such as one item, one test, or a block of time. The unit rules vary by service, so if a unit count looks off, ask billing to explain how it was calculated and what record supports it.

Do I have to pay the bill while the error is being corrected?

Ask billing to hold the account as under review. You may be able to pay the parts you agree are correct and dispute the rest, but policies differ, so ask first. Keep records of your request and any payment you make.
