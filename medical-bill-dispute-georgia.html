How to Dispute a Medical Bill in Georgia

Who handles what in Georgia

Georgia keeps insurance oversight in one place, which makes the first call easier to choose. Match your problem to the office below, then confirm on the office's own website that it handles your type of plan.

  • Insurer problems (denied claim, wrong payment, EOB that does not match the bill): the Office of Commissioner of Insurance and Safety Fire.
  • Provider billing practices you believe are unfair or deceptive: the Georgia Attorney General's consumer protection office.
  • Surprise bills from out-of-network emergency or ancillary providers: the federal CMS No Surprises Help Desk at 1-800-985-3059.
  • Medicaid billing questions: the state Medicaid agency, since providers who accept Medicaid generally may not bill beyond the Medicaid payment and any allowed copay.

Start with the bill, not the complaint

Most disputes are resolved, if at all, at the billing office. Call and ask for an itemized bill, then lay it beside your insurer's Explanation of Benefits (EOB). The EOB is not a bill; its patient responsibility line is the number the provider should be asking for.

Write down the date, the person's name, and what they promised. If a call does not fix the problem, follow up in writing so there is a dated record before you contact a regulator.

Federal rules that apply to Georgia residents

Federal protections apply in every state, and state law may add more. Check the regulators' websites for Georgia-specific protections rather than relying on a summary.

  • No Surprises Act: limits balance billing for emergency care and for out-of-network providers at in-network facilities, for most private plans. Ground ambulances are not covered by the federal law.
  • Fair Debt Collection Practices Act: if a collector contacts you, a written dispute within 30 days of the validation notice can pause collection until the debt is verified.
  • HIPAA right of access: you can request your medical and billing records, and providers must generally respond within 30 days.
  • Nonprofit hospitals must have a written financial assistance policy under federal tax rules.

An example: a charge that appears twice

Say a $400 imaging charge shows up twice for the same date. Circle both lines on the itemized bill, ask the billing office whether the second line was a repeat order or an entry error, and ask them to hold the account while they review.

If your insurer already paid, check the EOB before you pay anything. Billing you for an amount the insurer already covered is a common mix-up worth raising in writing.

Step-by-step

  1. Request the itemized billAsk the provider for every line item with dates and codes, not just a summary statement.
  2. Compare it to your EOBMatch each charge and the patient responsibility amount against what your insurer says you owe.
  3. Dispute in writingSend a dated letter naming the specific lines and asking for a response in a set time. Keep a copy.
  4. Escalate to the right officeUse the Commissioner's office for insurer issues, the Attorney General for billing practices, and CMS for surprise bills.

Frequently asked questions

Who do I complain to about a medical bill in Georgia?

It depends on who caused the problem. Insurer issues go to the Office of Commissioner of Insurance and Safety Fire. Provider billing practices can go to the Attorney General's consumer protection office. Surprise bill questions can go to the federal CMS No Surprises Help Desk. Check each website for how to file.

Does the No Surprises Act apply in Georgia?

Yes, it is a federal law and applies to most private health plans nationwide, including employer and marketplace plans. It does not apply to Medicare, Medicaid, TRICARE, VA, or Indian Health Service coverage, and it does not cover ground ambulance bills.

Does Georgia have extra protections beyond federal law?

State law may add protections, but this page does not describe them. Check the Commissioner's website or call the office to confirm what applies to your plan, because rules can differ for employer self-funded plans versus insured plans.

What if my Georgia medical bill went to collections?

Send a written dispute to the collector within 30 days of receiving the validation notice and keep proof of delivery. Under federal law the collector must stop collection until it verifies the debt. You can also submit a complaint to the CFPB.
