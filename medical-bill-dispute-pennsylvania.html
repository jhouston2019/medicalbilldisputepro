Disputing a Medical Bill in Pennsylvania, Starting With the Hospital

Hospital bills have two possible fixes

A big hospital bill can be wrong, or it can be correct but unaffordable. Those are different problems with different tools. Handle the error question first by asking for an itemized bill and comparing it to your EOB. Then handle the affordability question by asking about financial assistance.

Nonprofit hospital financial assistance

Under Internal Revenue Code section 501(r), tax-exempt hospital organizations must have a written financial assistance policy, limit charges to patients eligible for assistance, and make reasonable efforts before extraordinary collection actions. Many Pennsylvania hospitals are nonprofits, so this can matter to you.

Look for the policy on the hospital's website, often under billing or patient financial services. If you cannot find it, ask the billing office for a copy and the application.

  • Ask whether the hospital is tax-exempt.
  • Ask for the financial assistance policy and application in writing.
  • Ask the hospital to pause collection while your application is pending.
  • Send your application by a method that shows delivery.

Which Pennsylvania office for which problem

The Pennsylvania Insurance Department takes complaints about insurers and the plans it regulates. The Pennsylvania Attorney General's consumer protection office is where to raise unfair or deceptive billing practices. If you suspect a federal surprise billing violation, the CMS No Surprises Help Desk is 1-800-985-3059.

Checking the hospital's own numbers

Federal price transparency rules require hospitals to publish standard charges, including a consumer-friendly display of shoppable services. Comparing a charge on your bill with the hospital's published information can show whether something looks out of line, and gives you a concrete point to raise with the billing office.

A worked example

You receive a $6,000 hospital statement and a separate $900 bill from an out-of-network pathologist. First, ask for the itemized statement and check for repeated lines. Second, review the pathology bill under the No Surprises Act if the hospital was in network. Third, if the corrected total is still too high, apply for financial assistance. Doing these in order keeps each request clean.

State rules to verify

Pennsylvania may add protections of its own. Check the Insurance Department and Attorney General websites for current information about your plan type before citing anything.

Step-by-step

  1. Request the itemized statementAsk the hospital for line items, not just a summary balance.
  2. Check each separate billMatch the hospital and every physician bill to its EOB.
  3. Apply for assistanceAsk for the financial assistance application and a collection hold while it is pending.

Frequently asked questions

Do Pennsylvania hospitals have to offer financial assistance?

Tax-exempt hospital organizations must have a written financial assistance policy under federal tax rules. Whether a specific hospital is tax-exempt, and what its policy covers, varies. Ask the billing office or check its website.

Who handles insurance complaints in Pennsylvania?

The Pennsylvania Insurance Department takes complaints about insurers and the health plans it regulates. For provider billing practices, the Attorney General's consumer protection office may be appropriate. Self-funded employer plans are generally outside state insurance regulators' reach.

Can I dispute a hospital bill and apply for assistance at the same time?

Yes, and it is often sensible to do both. Dispute errors in writing and ask the hospital to pause collection while it reviews both the dispute and the application. Keep copies of everything you submit.

Is a separate pathologist or radiologist bill normal?

Often, yes, because those physicians may bill separately from the hospital. What matters is whether the provider is in network and whether the charge matches your EOB. If an out-of-network provider treated you at an in-network facility, the No Surprises Act may apply.

What if I cannot afford even the corrected bill?

Ask about financial assistance, payment plans and prompt-pay discounts, all in writing. Negotiation is common, though outcomes vary. Keep paying anything you agree to so the account stays in good standing.
