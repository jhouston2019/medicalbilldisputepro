Charged for Services or Supplies You Never Received: How to Dispute

Last reviewed 2026-11-02 · Billing errors
Short answerIf your bill lists a service, test, supply, or medication you did not receive, request an itemized bill and your medical records, compare each line to what was actually documented, and dispute the unsupported charges in writing. Wrong-patient entries and cancelled orders left on the account are possible explanations.

How can you be billed for something you never got?

A test can be ordered and then cancelled, but the charge stays on the account. A supply or medication can be recorded under the wrong patient. A procedure can be billed from a template of what usually happens rather than what actually happened. Most of these are errors rather than anything deliberate.

The effect on you is the same, so the response is the same: ask the provider to show documentation for each disputed line.

What should you check before contacting billing?

Gather what you already have. Memory is imperfect, especially after a stressful visit, so build your case from documents.

  • Your discharge papers or after-visit summary
  • The patient portal, which often lists tests, results, and medications
  • Your calendar and any notes from the day, plus a family member's recollection if they were with you
  • The EOB, to see which services your insurer was asked to pay for
  • Header details on the bill itself: your name, date of birth, account number, and dates of service

How do you request the records you need?

Ask for an itemized bill first. Then use your HIPAA right of access (45 C.F.R. § 164.524) to request your medical records, including billing records that are part of the designated record set. Providers must generally respond within 30 days.

Be specific about the dates and department. A request for the records of a particular imaging study or admission is faster to fill than a request for everything.

How do you compare the bill with the records?

Go line by line. For each charge, look for the order, the results or notes, and the time it was done. A charge with no matching record is the one to question.

  • A lab test with no result in the records
  • An imaging study with no report
  • A medication with no entry showing it was given
  • A room or monitoring charge for a day you were not there
  • A supply or device with no mention in any procedure note

What should your dispute say?

State the facts plainly. For example: I have no record of receiving the test listed on the date of service, and my records do not show it was performed. Please provide documentation that it was ordered and performed, or remove the charge. Include the account number, the line and amount, and ask for a response within 10 business days.

Be fair about the limits. Some items, like facility fees or supplies used in the room, may be legitimately billed even if you did not see them. Ask for an explanation rather than assuming an error.

What if your insurer already paid?

Tell your insurer as well, because a charge removed from the bill may also need to be corrected on the claim, which can change what you owe. If you believe the billing is deliberately false, rather than a mistake, that is a more serious matter that may call for a healthcare attorney or billing advocate.

Frequently asked questions

How do I prove I did not receive a service?

You usually cannot prove a negative, but the provider should be able to document what was done. Ask for the order, results, and notes for the disputed line. If there is no record the service was performed, that supports your request to remove the charge.

Can a hospital bill me for a test that was ordered but cancelled?

A cancelled test generally should not be billed, but the charge can remain on the account by mistake. Ask billing to confirm the test was performed and show the result. If it was cancelled, ask for the charge to be removed and a corrected bill sent.

What if the bill lists a visit date I was not there?

That could be a wrong date, a wrong-patient entry, or a mix-up with a different visit. Compare it with your portal and records, then ask billing to verify the date and patient. Our page on wrong dates and quantities covers how to catch these clerical errors.

Do I need a lawyer to dispute a charge for services I did not get?

Usually not. Most of these disputes are resolved by documenting the mismatch and asking billing to correct it. A healthcare attorney or billing advocate may help if the amount is large, the provider refuses to engage, or you suspect deliberate false billing.

What if I already paid for the service I did not receive?

Ask for a refund or credit in writing, include your payment receipt, and request a corrected statement. If you paid by card, keep records of the transaction. Notify your insurer too, in case it paid its share of the same charge.
