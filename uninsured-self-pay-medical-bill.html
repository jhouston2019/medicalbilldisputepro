Disputing a medical bill when you are uninsured or self-pay

Last reviewed 2026-11-02 · Billing situations

What is a good faith estimate?

Uninsured and self-pay patients are entitled to a good faith estimate of expected charges before scheduled care. It lists the services the provider or facility expects to provide and what they are expected to cost. It is an estimate, not a final price, but it creates a baseline you can compare against the final bill.

Keep the estimate. If you did not receive one for scheduled care, ask for it now and note in your dispute that you were not given one.

When can you use the federal dispute process?

If the final bill from a provider or facility is $400 or more above the good faith estimate from that same provider or facility, you may start the federal patient-provider dispute resolution process (42 U.S.C. § 300gg-136).

The process has a short filing window. Check cms.gov/nosurprises for the current deadline rather than relying on a number you heard elsewhere. Gather the estimate, the final bill and any records of changes in your care, since differences that were explained in advance may matter.

What should you check on a self-pay bill?

Even without insurance, a bill can contain errors, and there is no EOB to compare against. Use your itemized bill and your own records.

  • Duplicate lines for the same service on the same date.
  • Charges for services, tests or supplies you did not receive.
  • Quantities or units that look too high.
  • Whether the amount matches the good faith estimate, or the reason it does not.
  • Whether a higher visit level was billed than the care seemed to need.
  • Separate bills from physicians, labs and radiology that you were not told about.

What about financial assistance and discounts?

Tax-exempt hospital organizations must have a written financial assistance policy, limit charges to patients eligible for assistance, and make reasonable efforts before extraordinary collection actions (26 U.S.C. § 501(r)). Look for the policy on the hospital's website or ask the billing office for the application.

Whether or not you qualify for assistance, ask about a self-pay or prompt-pay discount and a payment plan. Get any agreement in writing and confirm that paying under it settles the account.

How should you negotiate and document it?

Be polite and specific. Tell the billing office which charges you question, what you can afford, and what outcome you want. Write down names, dates and what was offered, then follow up with a short email or letter.

Frequently asked questions

What if I was not given a good faith estimate?

Ask the provider or facility for one and note the date you asked. Uninsured and self-pay patients are entitled to an estimate before scheduled care. Without one, you can still dispute the bill by requesting an itemized statement, questioning specific charges and asking about financial assistance or a prompt-pay discount.

Does the $400 rule apply to every uninsured bill?

It applies to the federal patient-provider dispute resolution process, which looks at whether the final bill from a provider or facility is $400 or more above its good faith estimate. It is not a limit on what you can question otherwise. Check cms.gov/nosurprises for how the process works and the filing window.

Can I negotiate a medical bill if I have no insurance?

Yes. Many providers accept lower payments, offer discounts for paying promptly, or set up payment plans. Ask politely, explain what you can pay, and request any agreement in writing. Nonprofit hospitals must have financial assistance policies, so ask for the application before paying the full balance.

Do I have to pay while my dispute is open?

There is no single rule. Ask the provider in writing to pause collection while the dispute is reviewed, and keep proof of your request. If you choose to pay part, pay an amount you agree is correct and ask whether it affects your dispute. Keep every receipt.

Where can I complain if the hospital will not work with me?

You can contact your state attorney general about billing practices and your state insurance department for insurance-related issues. The CMS No Surprises Help Desk at 1-800-985-3059 can explain the federal process. If the account goes to a collector, the CFPB accepts complaints about debt collection.
