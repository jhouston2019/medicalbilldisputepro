Medical Bill Dispute Deadlines: Where They Come From

Last reviewed 2026-11-02 · How-to guides
Short answerThere is no single federal deadline for disputing a provider's medical bill. The deadlines that matter come from the bill itself, your insurer's EOB or denial notice, a collector's validation notice, or a specific federal process. Find each date on your documents, calendar them, and act early in writing.

Why is there no one deadline?

You may hear a round number such as 90 days, but there is no universal federal rule that sets one for disputing a provider's bill. Instead, many providers and collectors expect disputes within a short window printed on the bill. Treat that window seriously even if it is not a legal cutoff, and act promptly.

Where do the real deadlines come from?

Different documents carry different clocks. Pull out everything you have received and look for dates on each one.

  • The bill or statement: a due date and sometimes a dispute window
  • Your EOB or denial notice: the appeal deadline is printed there; many plans allow roughly six months, but check yours
  • A collector's validation notice: a written dispute within 30 days of receiving the notice can require the collector to stop collection until it verifies the debt (15 U.S.C. § 1692g)
  • The federal good faith estimate process: the filing window is short, so check the CMS site for the current deadline
  • Medicare notices: the appeal deadline is on your notice
  • HIPAA records requests: providers generally must respond within 30 days

What about deadlines you cannot find?

The statute of limitations on medical debt varies by state, and this page does not state a number. If a lawsuit has been filed or threatened, that is a good moment to speak with a healthcare attorney or your state attorney general's consumer office rather than relying on general information.

How can you protect yourself?

The safest approach is to write early. A prompt written dispute creates a dated record, even if the review takes longer.

  • Send your dispute in writing as soon as you spot a problem
  • Use tracking or a portal message so you can prove the date
  • Ask in writing for the account to be held while it is reviewed
  • Put every deadline on a calendar with a reminder a week ahead
  • Keep envelopes, notices, and screenshots in one folder

What if you think you missed a deadline?

Do not assume it is too late. Write to the provider or insurer explaining the error, ask whether an exception is possible, and keep a copy. Contact your state insurance department if your insurer will not consider an appeal, and ask for the reason in writing.

Step-by-step

  1. Gather every documentCollect the bill, EOB, denial notice, any collection letter, and any estimate you received.
  2. Find the datesLook for due dates, dispute windows, appeal deadlines, and the date you received each notice.
  3. Calendar each deadlineAdd the dates to a calendar with a reminder at least a week before each one.
  4. Send an early written disputeDo not wait for the last day. Put the dispute in writing and keep a dated copy.
  5. Keep proof of deliveryUse certified mail or a portal message so you can show when your request arrived.
  6. Ask for written confirmationRequest a reply that confirms the account is under review and not moving to collections.
  7. Follow up before each dateIf you have no answer, send a short reminder before the next deadline.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have only 90 days to dispute a medical bill?

No single federal 90-day rule applies to every provider bill. Some providers or plans have their own windows, which are usually printed on the bill or notice. Check each document you received, and act promptly rather than assuming a round number applies to your case.

Can I still dispute a bill after it goes to collections?

Yes. A third-party collector must send a validation notice, and a written dispute within 30 days of receiving it can require the collector to stop collection until it verifies the debt. You can also still raise billing errors with the original provider. Keep copies of everything you send.

Does disputing a bill pause the due date?

Not automatically. Ask the billing office in writing to hold the account while it reviews your dispute, and get their answer in writing. If the account is with a collector, the validation process described under the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act may stop collection until verification.

What if I missed my insurer's appeal deadline?

Contact the insurer in writing, explain what happened, and ask whether the appeal can still be considered. If you are not satisfied, your state insurance department may be able to tell you what options remain. Keep the denial notice, since it states the deadline that applied.
