Disputing a Medical Bill in New York

Last reviewed 2026-11-02 · Dispute a bill by state
Short answerIn New York, the Department of Financial Services is the insurance regulator that takes complaints about insurers, and the Attorney General handles consumer protection. If your insurer denied or underpaid a claim, use the plan's internal appeal and external review. Federal rules like the No Surprises Act apply alongside any state protections.

Think in terms of appeals first

In New York, many bill disputes trace back to a claim your insurer denied or paid at a lower rate. When that is the cause, fixing the claim fixes the bill, so the insurer's appeal process matters more than the provider's billing office.

Federal rules give insured patients an internal appeal followed by an independent external review. The deadline is printed on your denial notice or EOB; many plans allow roughly six months, but check yours.

Where each New York office fits

Use the office that matches the problem, and confirm on its website how to file.

  • Department of Financial Services: complaints about insurers and state-regulated health plans.
  • Attorney General: unfair or deceptive practices by providers, collectors or other businesses.
  • CMS No Surprises Help Desk, 1-800-985-3059: possible violations of the federal law.
  • Medicaid managed care and Medicare enrollees: use the plan's complaint process and the program's own help channels.

Three common New York scenarios

Scenario one: a specialist bills for the difference after your insurer pays an out-of-network rate. Ask whether the provider was out of network at an in-network facility; if so, the federal law may bar the extra bill unless you gave notice and consent.

Scenario two: a lab you never chose sends a bill. Ask your insurer whether the lab was in or out of network and what the EOB says you owe.

Scenario three: you were billed for a service the insurer says needed prior authorization. Ask the provider whether authorization was requested, and appeal with your doctor's help.

State protections: verify them

New York may offer rights beyond the federal ones, including around out-of-network care and external appeals. This page does not describe them because details depend on your plan. Check the Department of Financial Services website and cite what applies in your written dispute.

Keep the record clean

Keep each EOB, bill and letter, plus a log of every call with the date, the person and what was said. Send disputes by a method that gives proof of delivery. If a collector gets involved, a written dispute within 30 days of the validation notice can pause collection until the debt is verified.

Step-by-step

  1. Find the denial reasonRead the EOB or denial notice for the stated reason and the appeal deadline.
  2. File the internal appealSubmit it in writing with supporting records and ask your doctor's office to include a supporting note.
  3. Ask the provider to hold the billSend a dated request that the account not go to collections while the appeal is pending.
  4. Request external review if deniedFollow the instructions on your notice, and call the state regulator if you cannot tell how to file.

Frequently asked questions

Who regulates health insurers in New York?

The Department of Financial Services regulates insurers and takes consumer complaints about them. Self-funded employer plans are generally outside state insurance regulators' reach, so check your plan documents or ask HR if you are unsure which rules apply.

What is an external review?

After a plan's internal appeal, insured patients may request an independent external review of a denial. Check your denial notice or EOB for deadlines and instructions, and healthcare.gov explains the general process. The reviewer is separate from your insurer.

Does the federal No Surprises Act protect me in New York?

Yes, for most private plans, covering emergency services and out-of-network providers at in-network facilities. It does not cover Medicare, Medicaid, TRICARE, VA or Indian Health Service coverage, and it does not cover ground ambulances.

Should I pay while I wait for an appeal?

That is a judgment call that depends on your situation. Ask the provider in writing to hold the account while the claim is under appeal, and keep a copy. Do not ignore the bill, since collection notices can follow.
