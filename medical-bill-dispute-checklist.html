Medical Bill Dispute Checklist: Documents, Calls, and Timeline

Last reviewed 2026-11-02 · How-to guides
Short answerUse this checklist to organize a medical bill dispute: gather the itemized bill, EOB, and account details; log every call with date, name, and outcome; send a written dispute; and track follow-up dates. Keeping everything in one folder makes it easier to show what you said and when.

Which documents should you gather?

Create one folder, paper or digital, and add each of these as you receive it:

  • The itemized bill and every statement you received
  • Your EOB or Medicare Summary Notice for each date of service
  • Any denial or appeal letters from your insurer
  • Estimates, authorizations, and consent forms
  • Insurance card, account number, and provider contact details
  • Collection notices, if any, with the envelope or the date received

What should you write down on every call?

A call log turns memory into evidence. Use the same fields each time so it is easy to scan later.

  • Date and time of the call
  • Name and department of the person you spoke with
  • Phone number you called and any reference number
  • What you asked and what they said
  • What they promised and by what date
  • Your next step and the date to follow up

What does a simple timeline look like?

This is a personal pacing guide, not a legal deadline. Real deadlines come from your documents, so check each one.

Early on, request the itemized bill and your EOB and compare them. Soon after, call billing about specific errors and ask for a hold. Then send a written dispute with copies of your evidence and a reasonable response date. Finally, follow up in writing if you do not hear back, and escalate to your insurer's appeals process or a regulator if needed.

What should you check before sending anything?

Take a few minutes to review the package before it goes out.

  • Account number and date of service appear on every page
  • Each disputed charge is listed with its amount and reason
  • You are sending copies, not originals
  • You have asked for a specific outcome and a response date
  • You know how you will prove delivery

What should you do after you send it?

Add the response date to your calendar and keep the delivery receipt with your copy. If a reply arrives, compare it with your request and note anything still unresolved. If nothing arrives, send a short written follow-up and consider contacting your state regulator, as described in our complaint guide.

Step-by-step

  1. Create your folderSet up one place for every bill, notice, letter, and receipt related to the account.
  2. Collect core documentsAdd the itemized bill, EOB, denial letters, and any estimates or consent forms.
  3. Start a call logRecord the date, person, topic, promise, and next step for every conversation.
  4. Mark the errorsHighlight each questionable charge and write one line on why it looks wrong.
  5. Send a written disputeInclude account details, the charges in question, copies of evidence, and a response date.
  6. Track the datesPut due dates, appeal deadlines, and follow-up reminders on your calendar.
  7. Follow up and escalateIf there is no answer, write again and use the insurer's appeal process or a regulator.

Frequently asked questions

How long should I keep my medical bill dispute records?

Keep them at least until the account is resolved and you have written confirmation that the balance is settled or corrected. Many people keep them longer in case a charge reappears or a collector contacts them later. Store copies digitally if you can.

Do I need to log phone calls if I also send letters?

Yes. Calls are where promises are made, and those promises are easy to forget or dispute later. A short log with the date, name, and outcome helps you write accurate letters and shows the timeline if you need to escalate.

What if I am missing some of the documents?

Request them. Ask the provider for an itemized bill and your insurer for the EOB, and consider a written records request for chart documentation. Start with what you have and add the rest as it arrives.

Can I use this checklist for a Medicare or Medicaid bill?

Yes, with adjustments. Use the Medicare Summary Notice in place of an EOB for Medicare, and contact your state Medicaid agency for Medicaid questions, since providers who accept Medicaid generally may not bill you beyond the allowed amount. Keep the same records either way.
