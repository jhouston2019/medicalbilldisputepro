How to File a Medical Billing Complaint With Your State Regulator

Last reviewed 2026-11-02 · How-to guides
Short answerFile with the right agency: your state insurance department for insurer problems, your state attorney general for provider billing practices, the CMS No Surprises Help Desk (1-800-985-3059) for suspected No Surprises Act issues, and the CFPB for debt collectors. Include your documents, a short timeline, and the resolution you want.

Which agency handles what?

Sending a complaint to the wrong place can cost time. Match the problem to the agency:

  • State insurance department: complaints about your health insurer, such as a claim denial or processing error
  • State attorney general consumer protection office: complaints about provider billing practices
  • CMS No Surprises Help Desk, 1-800-985-3059: questions and complaints about possible No Surprises Act violations
  • CFPB: complaints about debt collectors and credit reporting
  • Medicare or your state Medicaid agency: problems with those programs

What should you try first?

Regulators usually expect you to have tried to resolve the problem directly. Contact the provider or insurer in writing, use the plan's appeal process if a claim was denied, and keep copies. A complaint is stronger when it shows that you gave the company a chance to fix the problem.

What should you include?

Keep it organized so a reviewer can understand it quickly.

  • Your contact information and the account or claim number
  • The provider's or insurer's name and address
  • Copies of the bill, EOB, denial notice, and letters you sent
  • A short timeline with dates
  • The specific outcome you are asking for

How do you write the timeline?

Use plain dated lines. For example: 'March 3: treated at the hospital. April 10: received a bill for $950. April 14: called billing, spoke with a representative, and asked for an itemized statement. April 28: sent a written dispute by certified mail.' Stick to facts and avoid guesses about motives.

What happens after you file?

Agencies differ in what they can do and how long they take. Some forward your complaint to the company and ask for a response. A complaint cannot promise a particular result. State law may add protections beyond federal rules, so check your regulator's website to see what applies where you live.

How do you find your state regulator?

The National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC) maintains a state web map that links to each state's insurance regulator. Your state attorney general's consumer protection page is usually easy to find with a quick search.

Step-by-step

  1. Identify the problemDecide whether the issue involves your insurer, the provider, a collector, or a government program.
  2. Try to resolve it directlyWrite to the responsible company, or use the insurer's appeal process, and keep copies.
  3. Gather your documentsCollect bills, EOBs, denial notices, letters, and call notes.
  4. Find the right regulatorUse the NAIC state map for the insurance department, and look up your attorney general's consumer office.
  5. Write a short timelineList the key dates and what happened on each one, using facts only.
  6. Submit the complaintUse the agency's online form, mail, or phone line, and attach copies, not originals.
  7. Save confirmation and follow upKeep the complaint number and check in if you do not hear back.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between the insurance department and the attorney general?

The state insurance department generally oversees insurers, so it is the usual place for problems with a denied or mishandled claim. The attorney general's consumer protection office generally deals with business practices, which can include provider billing and collection behavior. If you are unsure, start with the one that matches the company you are complaining about.

Will filing a complaint stop collection activity?

Not automatically. A complaint is separate from the validation rights you may have if a collector contacts you. Ask the provider in writing to hold the account while a complaint is pending, and respond to any collector's notice within the period it gives you.

Does the No Surprises Help Desk cover ground ambulance bills?

No. The federal No Surprises Act does not cover ground ambulance services, though it does cover air ambulance. For a ground ambulance bill, look to your state regulator, your plan's appeal process, and negotiation with the provider.

Do I need a lawyer to file a complaint?

No. Consumer complaints can be filed on your own, and most agencies provide online forms or phone assistance. A healthcare attorney may be useful if the amount is large or a lawsuit has been filed, but is not required to submit a regulator complaint.
