Disputing a Medical Bill in Florida

Two insurance offices and one consumer office

Florida splits insurance oversight between two agencies, which confuses many people. Think of it as a rule-maker and a help line, then add the Attorney General for billing practices.

  • Office of Insurance Regulation: oversees insurers and their products and rates; it is generally not where you file an individual claim dispute.
  • Department of Financial Services consumer helpline: the usual starting point when your insurer mishandled a claim or an appeal.
  • Attorney General consumer protection: for unfair or deceptive billing practices by providers or collectors.
  • CMS No Surprises Help Desk, 1-800-985-3059: for balance billing that may violate the federal law.

Emergency room bills are the common Florida trigger

Large hospitals and busy emergency rooms are where many people first see a confusing bill. An ER visit often produces separate bills: one from the hospital for the facility, and another from the physician group that staffed the department.

For most private plans, the No Surprises Act protects you from balance billing by out-of-network emergency providers and generally limits cost sharing to in-network levels. If a bill asks for more than your EOB says you owe, ask the provider to re-process it under that rule.

What to look for on a Florida ER bill

Request an itemized bill and check these points before you call.

  • The emergency visit level. Levels run from 99281 to 99285, and a charge at the top level for a minor problem is worth questioning.
  • Two separate bills for the same visit, and whether each reflects what your EOB allowed.
  • Any line that repeats on the same date.
  • Whether your insurer already paid and the provider is billing you for the full amount anyway.

Other federal tools that apply in Florida

A collector's written validation notice gives you 30 days from receipt to dispute in writing under the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, and collection must pause until the debt is verified. You can also complain to the CFPB. Nonprofit hospitals must have a written financial assistance policy, usually posted on the hospital's website.

Florida law may add protections beyond these. Check the Department of Financial Services and Attorney General websites for what applies to your plan.

Step-by-step

  1. Get the itemized billAsk both the hospital and any physician group for line-item bills.
  2. Match it to the EOBCompare patient responsibility on each EOB to each bill.
  3. Write to the providerName the lines in question, state what you believe is wrong, and ask for a response within 10 business days.
  4. Call the right officeUse the DFS helpline for insurer issues, the Attorney General for billing practices, or CMS for surprise billing.

Frequently asked questions

Who do I call about a medical bill problem in Florida?

If your insurer is the issue, the Department of Financial Services consumer helpline is the usual starting point. For provider billing practices, the Attorney General's consumer protection office may help. For surprise billing under federal law, call the CMS No Surprises Help Desk.

What does the Florida Office of Insurance Regulation do?

It oversees insurers and their products. It is generally not the office that resolves an individual billing disagreement, so most consumers start with the Department of Financial Services helpline. Confirm on the agency websites which office handles your type of complaint.

I got two bills after an ER visit. Is that an error?

Not necessarily. Hospitals and the physician groups staffing the ER often bill separately. Still, compare each to your EOB, check for repeated charges, and confirm neither bill exceeds what you owe under your plan or the No Surprises Act.

Does the No Surprises Act cover Florida ground ambulances?

No. The federal law covers air ambulance services but not ground ambulances. For ground ambulance bills, review your plan terms, appeal through your insurer, ask the state regulator about state protections, and try negotiating.
