Disputing a Medical Bill in Michigan: Start With Your Records

Last reviewed 2026-11-02 · Dispute a bill by state
Short answerIn Michigan, begin by requesting your itemized bill and your medical records under HIPAA, since providers must generally respond within 30 days. Insurer complaints go to the Department of Insurance and Financial Services, and billing practices can go to the Attorney General. Federal rules apply alongside any state protections.

Why records come before arguments

You cannot argue that a service was not provided, or was billed at the wrong level, without seeing what the chart says. The HIPAA right of access lets patients request their medical records and, generally, billing records that are part of the designated record set. Providers must generally respond within 30 days.

What to ask for

Make the request in writing and name the date of service.

  • The itemized bill with dates, descriptions and codes.
  • The records for the visit, such as visit notes and orders.
  • Any record showing which services were actually performed.
  • Your account ledger, showing payments and adjustments.

Matching the chart to the bill

Once the records arrive, read the bill against them. Look for services billed that the notes do not mention, a visit level that seems higher than the complexity described, the wrong date or patient, and quantities that do not match. These are the kinds of clerical and coding errors a written dispute can point to.

You do not need to interpret clinical details. Note the mismatch, quote the line, and ask the provider to explain or correct it.

Which Michigan office to contact

The Michigan Department of Insurance and Financial Services takes consumer complaints about insurers and the plans it regulates. For unfair or deceptive billing practices, the Michigan Attorney General's consumer protection office may help. For surprise billing under federal law, the CMS No Surprises Help Desk is 1-800-985-3059.

Federal and state rules

The No Surprises Act protects most privately insured patients in emergencies and at in-network facilities, with ground ambulances not covered. If a collector contacts you, a written dispute within 30 days of the validation notice can pause collection. Michigan law may add protections, so check the department's website for your plan type.

Step-by-step

  1. Decide what you are checkingPick the one or two charges that look wrong so your records request stays focused and easy for the provider to answer.
  2. Send a written records requestAddress it to the provider's health information department and name the date of service and the records you want.
  3. Calendar the 30-day markProviders must generally respond within 30 days, so note the date you sent the request and follow up if it passes.
  4. Mark mismatchesCompare each billed line to the notes and highlight services, dates or quantities that do not line up.
  5. Dispute in writingQuote the specific lines, describe the mismatch, and ask for a corrected bill or an explanation within 10 business days.
  6. Escalate if neededTake unresolved insurer issues to the state insurance regulator and unresolved billing practices to the Attorney General.

Frequently asked questions

How do I request my medical records in Michigan?

Send a written request to the provider's records or health information department under your HIPAA right of access. Name the date of service and ask for billing records too. Providers must generally respond within 30 days.

Are billing records part of my medical record?

Billing records that are part of the provider's designated record set are generally covered by the right of access. Ask for the itemized bill and account ledger by name so there is no confusion about what you want.

Who takes insurance complaints in Michigan?

The Department of Insurance and Financial Services takes consumer complaints about insurers. If your employer self-funds your plan, the state regulator may not have authority, so check your plan documents or ask HR which appeal route applies.

What if the chart says a service was never performed?

Point out the mismatch in writing, quote the billed line, and ask the provider to explain or remove it. If the provider does not respond, escalate to your insurer or the proper regulator and keep copies of everything.
